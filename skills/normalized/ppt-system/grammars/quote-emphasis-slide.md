# Quote / Emphasis Slide Grammar

## Use Case

Deliver a single high-impact statement that the audience should remember. The slide stops the flow, creates a moment of focus, and anchors a key idea.

Typical scenarios:
- Opening hook (hero / title slide)
- Key insight or provocative claim
- Customer quote or testimonial
- Section divider with thematic statement
- Closing takeaway

## Narrative Role

- **emphasis** — isolates and amplifies one statement
- **introduction** — sets the tone and frames the presentation (hero variant)
- **transition** — signals a section change with a thematic anchor
- **summary** — leaves the audience with one consolidated thought

## Layout Pattern

**Full-screen text or image overlay** — the statement fills the primary visual space.

Primary structures:

**Text-only variant:**
- Dominant typography centered or aligned to a strong grid line
- Large whitespace surrounding the text
- Optional: subtle rule line or minimal geometric accent

**Image overlay variant:**
- Full-bleed background image
- Text overlaid with sufficient contrast (dark overlay or text shadow)
- Image must support, not compete with, the text

**Hero variant (opening slide):**
- Title + subtitle + visual anchor
- Presentation identity established through typography and color
- The most branded/produced slide in the deck

## Information Density

**Very low.** This is the lowest-density slide type.

Maximum:
- 1 primary statement (headline or quote)
- 1 attribution line or subtitle (optional)
- 1 visual element (background image, icon, or geometric accent)
- Zero bullet points
- Zero explanatory text

## Content Constraints

- The statement must be **self-contained** — it lands without prior context
- Ideal length: 3–12 words for a headline, 10–30 words for a quote
- Attribution (if any) is minimal — name, title, company on one line
- No hedging, no qualifiers — the statement is declarative
- If used as a section divider, the statement should encapsulate the upcoming section's theme

## Cognitive Goal

Create a **pause with impact**. The audience stops scanning and receives one idea with full attention. For the opening hero: establish immediate clarity and emotional direction.

## Visual Expectations

- The text is the undisputed focal point — nothing competes
- Generous whitespace amplifies the statement's weight
- Typography carries the emotional tone (weight, size, letter-spacing)
- If an image is used, it creates mood without distracting
- The slide feels intentional and composed, not empty

## Avoid

- Explanatory paragraphs
- Multiple competing text elements
- Decorative clutter that dilutes focus
- Low-contrast text on images
- Treating this as a "filler" slide — it should carry weight
