# Rhythm Policy

## Purpose

Control the **information density cadence** across a presentation. Rhythm prevents cognitive overload by alternating between high-density content slides and low-density breathing space.

## Source

Derived from the orchestrator's **Presentation Philosophy** (specifically "Simplicity reduces cognitive load") and the principle of cognitive variation.

---

## Core Rhythm Rules

### 1. Density Alternation

Information density must vary across consecutive slides. A high-density slide should be followed by a medium or low-density slide.

**Density scale:**
| Level | Description | Typical Grammar |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Very Low | 1 statement, no data | Quote/Emphasis |
| Low | 1 concept, minimal detail | Quote/Emphasis, Process (3 steps) |
| Medium | Multiple structured items, data present | Comparison, Metrics, Framework/Model |
| High | Dense data, multiple visual elements | Metrics (multi-card), Framework (complex) |

### 2. Consecutive Density Limit

**Maximum 2 consecutive Medium+ density slides.** After two slides at Medium or above, a Low or Very Low density slide must intervene as a "breathing page."

### 3. Breathing Page Frequency

A breathing page (Quote/Emphasis or low-density Process) should appear at minimum every **4 slides**. This provides a cognitive reset point.

Breathing page types:
- Full-screen quote or key statement
- Section divider with thematic anchor
- Single-image slide with minimal overlay text
- Transition slide signaling a topic shift

---

## Section-Level Rhythm

### Opening Section (first 3–5 slides)

Pattern: **Hook → Context → Map**
- Very Low (hook) → Medium (context/framework) → Medium (agenda/map)

Avoid opening with data-heavy slides — establish emotional and conceptual grounding first.

### Body Sections (varies by deck length)

Pattern: **Claim → Evidence → Bridge**
- Medium (claim/framework) → Medium/High (evidence/metrics) → Low/Very Low (bridge)

Each body section should end with a breathing page before transitioning to the next section.

### Closing Section (last 2–3 slides)

Pattern: **Synthesis → Call to Action**
- Medium (summary/comparison) → Very Low (final statement)

The final slide should be a single takeaway — the lowest density in the deck.

---

## Cognitive Momentum

Momentum is managed through **progressive clarity**:

1. **Early deck**: broader concepts, higher ambiguity tolerated, lower density
2. **Mid deck**: increasing specificity, evidence introduced, density peaks
3. **Late deck**: synthesis and convergence, density decreases toward resolution

The audience's cognitive load should feel like a wave, not a staircase:
- Gradual build, not abrupt density spikes
- Valleys (breathing pages) between peaks (evidence sections)
- Resolution at the end

---

## Deck-Length Adjustments

| Deck Length | Breathing Pages | Max Consecutive Medium+ |
|------------|----------------|------------------------|
| 5–10 slides | Every 3–4 slides | 2 |
| 10–20 slides | Every 4 slides | 2 |
| 20–40 slides | Every 4–5 slides + section dividers | 3 |
| 40+ slides | Every 5 slides + chapter structure | 3 |

---

## Implementation

The orchestrator reads this policy during **Stage 2: Narrative Planning** and **Stage 3: Slide Grammar Assignment**:

1. After narrative arc is planned, assign density levels to each planned slide
2. Validate against consecutive density limits
3. Insert breathing pages where gaps exceed threshold
4. Re-check after grammar assignment to ensure grammar types match assigned density levels
